New in 1.9.6

Any label, several to a page, in color

Sheet labels
A sheet of blank label stock and an ordinary desktop laser printer now work with any fixed‑shape label the app knows, several to a page, with cut marks to follow. The layouts stop requiring the printer they were authored for: the 50×80mm two‑track label existed only for M110 owners until this. US Letter and A4 both.
Color
The first time a dj label can carry color. Colored dots in seven shapes, placed by tapping the preview, for mood‑marking. On a label you sized yourself, every element takes a color by tapping it, and one record in a crate can be overridden at review without touching the design. The QR stays black.
Your own label size
Drag a label by its corner against a grid - totaly customizable. Magnet lines mark where the grid gains or loses a column, numbered with the count they begin, and a wheel beside it lists every count a page can hold.
A shelf you can read by tempo
The BPM filter is a histogram of your own collection. The range runs from your slowest record to your fastest, empty gaps are drawn because a gap is worth knowing about, and a bar’s height is exactly how many records hold that bpm. Marking a record printed by hand stores the tempos its label would have carried, so those records turn up in a search.
  • On the 2.4" roll, each section of a label is a height in millimetres instead of a share of a page that has no end, so making one band taller no longer shrinks the others. The card says how long the label prints.
  • Crates hold 100 records, up from 50, so a crate can fill a sheet.
  • A coverage ring and a percentage on every artist and every letter, so you can see how much of a shelf is labelled without opening it.
  • Holding the price arrow takes about a second and a half to go from $5 to $80, instead of eleven.
  • Cancel and Save in the tune editor are reachable while approving a crate.
  • Densely filled text 45 labels resolve more accurately.
  • Collection artwork loads from the picture a scan already worked out, instead of trying Discogs.
